PROJECT 01

Sales & Profitability Analysis

Turned a 180K-row supply-chain transactions dataset into a decision-ready Power BI report, fixing data-quality problems in SQL before building a single visual.

The business questions

  • Is the business becoming more profitable, or only selling more?
  • Are discounts helping the business make money, or costing too much margin?
  • Which products and categories really drive profit, not just transactions?

What I did

  • Profiled and cleaned the source data, fixing inconsistent values, invalid records, missing information and formatting problems.
  • Built a Bronze → Silver → Gold workflow in PostgreSQL to clean, standardize and prepare the data.
  • Designed a star schema with fact and dimension tables, fixing the grain so every calculation is consistent.
  • Created SQL views for profitability, product and category performance, and discount analysis.
  • Wrote DAX measures and a 3-page interactive report: executive overview, discount analysis, and product and category performance.
  • Validated and reconciled figures against the source, and documented a data limitation (the final year holds only 37 days, so it is excluded from year-on-year comparisons).

PROJECT 02

Operations & Revenue Analysis (AdventureWorks)

Scoped a stakeholder email into KPIs, then analysed 121,253 sales order lines (FY2018 to FY2020) to explain why sales doubled while margin slipped, and where the profit was really being lost.

Key findings

  • Growth with margin pressure: sales rose from $23.9M to $51.9M, while margin fell from 12.7% to 11.2%.
  • Revenue and profit sit in different places: Australia is about 10% of sales but 29% of gross profit; Central, Southeast and Northeast are 21% of sales at roughly a 2% margin.
  • The losses are a channel problem, not a product problem: Touring Bikes lose money through resellers (-11.8% margin) but earn 37.8% online. Discounting was tested and ruled out as the cause.

What I did

  • Turned a broad request into scoped objectives, KPIs and a defined analysis period.
  • Profiled the data in PostgreSQL, including key, orphan-record, missing-value and financial reconciliation checks, and documented the limitations I found.
  • Cleaned it through a Bronze → Silver → Gold pipeline and modeled a star schema with sentinel keys and role-playing dates.
  • Built a Power BI semantic model with central DAX measures and fiscal-calendar time intelligence.
  • Wrote prioritized recommendations, such as repricing reseller finished-bike lines against standard cost.

How I Work

My Approach to Data Analysis

A reliable report starts long before the first chart. I make sure the data can support the answer.

01. Start with the Decision

Clarify what the stakeholder needs to know, why it matters, and what decision the analysis supports.

02. Profile and Clean the Data

Check types, missing values, duplicates and invalid records, then fix them in a repeatable SQL pipeline.

03. Model for Accuracy

Set the correct grain and build fact and dimension tables so every measure calculates the same way.

04. Reconcile, Then Communicate

Validate figures against the source before reporting, then present findings, limitations and next steps clearly.
